A spotted star fools a transit measurement

When a planet crosses its star, the dip in light tells us the planet's size. But the star is not a uniform disk: cool spots are dimmer than the rest. Even when the planet never touches a spot, the spots change how deep the dip looks. This is the transit light source effect.

What to notice

  1. The dashed curve is the same transit of a spotless star. The solid curve is the spotted star. The planet is identical in both.
  2. The planet blocks only clean surface, but the spots have already made the whole star dimmer. The planet's shadow is the same brightness and now a larger fraction of what is left, so the dip looks deeper.
  3. Drag Spot size to zero and the two curves meet. The M and K presets give nearly the same error for the same spots, because what matters is how much dimmer the spots are than the star, and that is similar for these two.
